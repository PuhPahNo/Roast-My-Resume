A bad response rate is a clue, not a diagnosis

There is no universal application count that proves a resume is broken. A narrow search, a difficult market, visa requirements, location, compensation, and experience level all affect the math. Still, repeated silence from well-matched applications is a good reason to audit the document before sending another fifty copies.

Start with the distinction that matters: are employers rejecting your background, or can they not see the evidence for it? A resume cannot change your years of experience. It can make the experience you already have easier to find and harder to dismiss.

The three fixes with the highest return

1. Replace duties with evidence

“Managed customer accounts” only names the assignment. Add the scale, what you changed, and the result: portfolio size, renewal rate, response time, revenue, volume, or another fact you can support. See five before-and-after resume roast examples.

2. Make the target obvious

The first few lines should make your role, level, specialty, and strongest evidence clear. Delete broad adjectives such as “hardworking” and “results-driven.” They take space without narrowing the reader's understanding.

3. Check the extracted text

Copy the document into a plain-text editor. If dates, headings, and job descriptions arrive in a strange order, simplify the layout before assuming every hiring system will reconstruct it correctly. Our ATS guide explains the practical limits without pretending every system works alike.

One useful outside standard

The U.S. Department of Labor-sponsored CareerOneStop guide recommends connecting work to context and outcomes, then using relevant language from the job posting. That is a better test than stuffing a skills section with every term you can find. Read the work-experience guidance.

Quick answers

Can I fix a cooked resume in one weekend?

Often, yes—if the underlying experience is relevant and the main problem is vague writing or structure. Start with the summary and the top two bullets under each recent role.

Should I use a designed template?

Use design only when it improves scanning without scrambling the text order. Creative fields may value stronger visual presentation; conventional roles usually benefit from restraint.
