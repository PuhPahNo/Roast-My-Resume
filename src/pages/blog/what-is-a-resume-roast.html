Resume roast, defined

Direct feedback only works when the fix is useful

A resume roast uses humor to make weak writing obvious. The joke is optional. The evidence and correction are not.

Reviewed by RoastMyResume · Updated August 30, 2026

What “roast” means here

A resume roast is a critique written in a sharper voice than a traditional review. It calls out vague claims, weak evidence, repetition, and confusing structure without dressing the problem up in polite filler.

Useful sharpness stays aimed at the document. It does not attack a person's identity, background, age, disability, name, or protected traits. It also does not invent a problem because the writer needs another joke.

The three parts of a good roast

1. Quote or identify the weak text

“Worked on marketing campaigns” is specific enough to inspect. “Your experience section is bad” is not.

2. Explain what the reader cannot learn

The line does not establish the channel, your ownership, scale, audience, or result. That diagnosis is more useful than calling it boring.

3. Give a truthful revision path

Ask for the missing facts: which campaigns, what you owned, the size of the audience or budget, and what changed. A tool should show the shape of a stronger line without making up your numbers.

Small example

Before: “Responsible for onboarding new customers.”

The roast: “Responsible for” tells us the task arrived in your inbox. It does not tell us whether you improved the process or merely survived it.

The fix: Add volume, ownership, and an outcome you can defend—for example, the number of customers, time to activation, completion rate, or support tickets reduced.

What a text-based tool misses

Roast My Resume extracts text from the uploaded file and sends that text to a language model. Text extraction can drop fonts, color, spacing, and the visual relationship between columns. The tool should not claim that a margin is uneven or a page is visually crowded when it cannot see the rendered document.

The same boundary applies to ATS feedback. A general readability check can flag conventional headings and strange extracted order; it cannot reproduce a specific employer's system. Read the exact scoring methodology.

How to use the result

  1. Reject any comment that depends on a visual detail the model did not receive.
  2. Reject any rewrite that adds a fact you cannot verify.
  3. Keep criticism that makes a real reader's question obvious.
  4. Write the final line in your own voice and check it against the target role.
  5. Export the document and inspect the finished page yourself.
