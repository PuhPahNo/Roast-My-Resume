Work history

Explain the gap without making it the headline

Keep the dates accurate, share only the context you choose, and return the reader's attention to your current qualifications.

Reviewed by RoastMyResume · Updated August 30, 2026

Not every gap needs its own entry

A short transition between roles may be clear from year-and-month dates and need no explanation. Add a separate line when a longer break makes the timeline confusing, when the activity during it is relevant, or when naming it helps you present the history more accurately.

There is no universal six-month cutoff and no script that controls how every employer will react.

Use a simple dated entry

A useful entry can contain three parts: a neutral label, dates, and one short line of relevant context. Availability belongs in a cover letter or conversation unless it resolves a specific concern.

Career Break · March 2024–January 2025

Provided full-time family care; completed continuing education in Salesforce administration before returning to the job search.

The example is fictional. Do not claim training, freelance work, or projects you did not complete.

Examples for common situations

Layoff

“Career transition · September 2025–present — Position eliminated during a company-wide reduction; completed two contract data-cleanup projects.” Only include the contract work if it was real.

Caregiving

“Family caregiving leave · 2024–2025.” That may be enough. You do not owe a diagnosis, relationship, or account of someone else's health.

Health

“Personal leave · January–August 2025.” Keep medical information private unless you choose to discuss it. Focus the rest of the resume on current qualifications.

Education

List a degree or formal program in Education with its dates. Add a gap entry only if the timeline remains unclear or other relevant activity belongs there.

Travel or sabbatical

“Planned sabbatical · May–December 2025 — Traveled in South America and maintained two existing freelance design clients.” Do not manufacture professional activity to make the break look busier.

Do not solve the gap with inaccurate dates

Changing end dates, removing all dates, or inventing a consulting business creates a factual problem. Accurate years and months plus a neutral line are easier to explain.

Bring the summary back to the role

If the break is recent and important to the transition, the summary can mention it once: “Customer-success manager returning after a caregiving break, with prior ownership of a $3.1M renewal portfolio.” The rest should establish the work you can do now. See more resume summary examples.

Prepare the spoken version

An interviewer may ask about the period. Prepare two or three accurate sentences: what the break was, what is relevant from it, and why the role fits now. You can protect private information and still answer directly.

Check the entire timeline

The free roast can flag unclear dates and weak evidence in the extracted text. It cannot predict an employer's reaction.
