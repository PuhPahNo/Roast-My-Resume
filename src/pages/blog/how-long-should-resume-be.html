Resume editing

How long should a resume be?

Use the shortest version that preserves the evidence relevant to the target role. For many people that is one page; for many others it is two.

Reviewed by RoastMyResume · Updated August 30, 2026

There is no reliable seven-year rule

Years of experience are a weak shortcut for document length. A recent graduate with substantial projects, service, and technical work may need more space than an experienced person targeting a narrow role. A senior operator may have twenty years of work but need only a few achievements from the last decade.

The better test is relevance. Does each section help the reader evaluate you for this opening?

One page works when the story fits

Use one page when you can show your target, recent experience, strongest evidence, education, and relevant skills without tiny text or missing context. It is often a good fit for students, recent graduates, and candidates with a focused work history, but it is not a rule tied to an age or graduation date.

A second page is useful when it earns its place

Use two pages when the second page contains material that directly strengthens the application: another relevant role, technical projects, publications, certifications, major volunteer leadership, or achievements that would otherwise be reduced to a vague list.

Do not force two pages to look senior. Do not force one page by shrinking the body type until it is unpleasant to read. Edit content before editing legibility.

The second-page test

  • Would removing this item hide evidence required by the posting?
  • Is the information different from what page one already proves?
  • Is it recent enough—or unusually relevant enough—to help?
  • Can the item be shortened without losing the result or context?
  • Does the application ask for a special format or complete work history?

If most second-page content fails those questions, cut or compress it.

Exceptions are not edge cases

Academic CVs, medical CVs, federal resumes, portfolios, and applications with explicit instructions can follow different length and content conventions. Use the requested format. A general web article should not override the employer or institution receiving the document.

In the UK and much of Europe, a job-search CV often serves the same purpose as a U.S. resume, while an academic CV is a longer record. Our CV guide explains that distinction.

How to cut length without losing proof

  1. Start with repetition. If three roles prove the same skill, keep the strongest example and shorten the others.
  2. Compress older work. Relevant older roles can stay, but they rarely need the same detail as recent ones.
  3. Remove setup language. “In order to,” “successfully,” and “responsible for” usually add length without evidence.
  4. Delete generic sections. Unsupported soft-skill lists and broad objectives are easier to cut than specific achievements.
  5. Protect readability. Keep comfortable type, margins, spacing, and a clear hierarchy.

Check the result, not only the page count

Read the exported file at normal size. Check that dates align, headings are consistent, and no lonely heading falls at the bottom of page one. Then copy the text into a plain editor to confirm that the reading order survives extraction.
