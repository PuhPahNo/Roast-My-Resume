How the tool works

What an AI resume roaster can—and cannot—tell you

Automated feedback is good at finding vague writing and missing evidence. It is much less useful when people treat one score as a hiring decision.

Reviewed by RoastMyResume · Updated August 30, 2026

Start with the right job for the tool

An AI resume roaster is a first-pass editor. It can read the text in your document, identify weak phrasing, and return a consistent checklist quickly. That makes it useful before you ask a mentor, recruiter, or colleague to spend time on the details.

It is not a recruiter. It does not know the unwritten preferences of a hiring manager, and it cannot prove that a revision will get more interviews.

What it can review well

Vague bullets

“Helped with sales reporting” leaves the reader with questions. A useful critique should ask what you owned, who used the work, and what changed. Even when the exact number is unavailable, scope and outcome make the line more informative.

Evidence that is already on the page

The tool can notice whether claims have quantities, time frames, scale, or a concrete result. It should never invent those details. A blank number is better than a fake one.

Basic structure and readability

Conventional headings, consistent dates, focused summaries, and concise bullets are straightforward to check from extracted text. The review can also flag sections that are unusually dense or repetitive.

A repeatable second look

After an edit, running the same checklist again can expose a new problem you introduced. Treat the score as a rough diagnostic, not as an objective grade.

What it cannot know

  • The job's priorities unless you compare your resume with the actual posting yourself.
  • The visual page after text extraction. Columns, spacing, colors, and visual hierarchy may not reach the model as they appear to a person.
  • A particular ATS's behavior. Applicant-tracking systems differ by vendor, employer configuration, and workflow.
  • Whether a claim is true. You remain responsible for every fact.
  • Whether a hiring team will interview you. Timing, competition, referrals, location, and many other factors sit outside the document.

How Roast My Resume handles a file

The application extracts readable text from a PDF, DOCX, or TXT file and sends that text to Groq for the automated review. The temporary uploaded file is removed after the request. Because the model receives extracted text, our “ATS” score is a general readability check—not a simulation of Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, or another employer system.

The exact scoring categories, prompt boundaries, and data handling are documented in our methodology and privacy policy.

A sensible workflow

  1. Save the job posting and underline the work it actually asks you to do.
  2. Run a first-pass roast to find unclear writing and thin evidence.
  3. Accept only suggestions that are accurate and relevant to that posting.
  4. Export the revision and read the rendered document yourself.
  5. Ask someone familiar with the role to challenge the final story.

If your document uses an unusual layout, copy its text into a plain-text editor. If the reading order is scrambled there, simplify the layout before submitting it.
